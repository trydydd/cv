## Design Context

### Users
Primary: hiring managers, recruiters, interviewers, professional contacts — time-constrained and evaluative, should land on the best resume immediately. Secondary: technical reviewers and curious peers who want to inspect the multi-model resume experiment, compare variants, and understand how outputs were produced.

### Brand Personality
Precise, transparent, ambitious — and unmistakably human. A polished personal portfolio crossed with a working lab that someone clearly loves running: confident enough for non-technical viewers, explicit enough for technical reviewers, warm enough that a visitor remembers the person behind the letterhead. The brand mark is "Willard Hucks"; the experiment is a labeled section, not the identity. Wit lives at the edges (colophon, 404, gallery ledes, empty states) and never in the resume content or provenance data. Physical scene reference: a sunlit study, not a sterile review room — one excellent printed resume on the desk, beside a well-thumbed lab notebook annotated in plum and ochre ink, with a dry note in the margin.

### Aesthetic Direction
Print/editorial with a pulse: paper surfaces, hairline rules, and typographic hierarchy instead of cards, pills, gradients, or floating shadows — but set bravely, not timidly. Light theme, OKLCH tokens: plum-tinted paper background (`oklch(0.972 0.007 340)`), sheet-white document surfaces, deep plum primary (`oklch(0.42 0.165 340)`) used generously — plum display headlines and thick plum rules at key moments are encouraged, not rationed. One secondary annotation ink, ochre (`oklch(0.50 0.105 75)`), reserved for margin asides and editorial marks. Typography: Besley (Clarendon serif) for display, headings, the brand mark, and italic asides — let its character show at large sizes; Atkinson Hyperlegible for interface and body text; JetBrains Mono strictly for provenance data (hashes, model IDs, parameters) — all-caps mono labels are a provenance device, not a default section-heading style. Editorial devices carry the personality: specimen numbering (№ 01…) on ledger rows, short italic margin asides, footnote-style provenance, a colophon footer written like a person. Anti-references: generic beige AI portfolio template, pill buttons and badge chips, identical card grids, gradient washes, dense dashboards/developer-tool chrome on the general-viewer path — and equally, sterile lab minimalism where every label is small uppercase mono and no sentence sounds like a human wrote it.

### Design Principles
1. Route by intent, then disappear: `Resume` gets the gold resume with minimum friction; `Resume Gallery` gets the lab.
2. Trust through provenance: model, prompt, input, and output metadata visible where it helps decision-making.
3. Voice is part of the design system: microcopy is first-person, specific, and dryly funny at the edges — concrete numbers over grand claims ("eleven models, one YAML file" beats "cutting-edge AI").
4. Configuration over code churn: site behavior follows YAML registries.
5. Resume first, experiment second: warmth and wit support credibility, never distract from the candidate — the resume sheet itself stays straight-faced.
6. Target WCAG 2.2 AA; do not rely on color alone for tier/status; respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; personality is never purchased at accessibility's expense.
